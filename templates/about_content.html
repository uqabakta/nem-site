The name

An ergodic system is one where a single long-run trajectory reveals the same statistics as averaging across many possible trajectories — the assumption quietly underpinning Monte Carlo simulation, and one that becomes interestingly fragile in a market shaped by price spikes, structural breaks, and the rapid entry of storage and renewables. It felt like the right name for a practice built on taking that assumption seriously, rather than for granted.

Background

The analysis published here is produced by a quantitative analyst with two PhDs in computational physics and close to a decade of experience in stochastic simulation, probabilistic forecasting, and energy market analytics in the Australian National Electricity Market. The methods used across these dashboards — Monte Carlo price simulation, spike and negative-price probability modelling, capture-rate and value-factor analysis for wind, solar and storage — are the same class of techniques used professionally in market risk and generation asset analysis, applied here entirely to public data.
